## 🟢 The Big Idea (ELI5)

The **Quadratic Formula** is the **universal hammer**. It solves *any* quadratic — even ugly ones that don't factor.

For `ax² + bx + c = 0`:

> ## **x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a**

Plug in a, b, c. Out come the solutions. Always works.

It's a long formula but it's just **a recipe with 6 numbers** to plug in. Once you know it cold, you're never stuck on a quadratic again.

---

## 📖 Vocab You Need

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Quadratic formula** | x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a |
| **Discriminant** | The part under the square root: **b² − 4ac**. Tells you how many real solutions: + = 2, 0 = 1, − = none. |
| **a, b, c** | The coefficients in `ax² + bx + c = 0` |
| **Two solutions** | Quadratics typically have two answers (the ±) |

---

## 🧾 The Recipe

For `ax² + bx + c = 0`:

1. **Get it into standard form** (one side = 0).
2. **Identify a, b, c** carefully — **with their signs**.
3. **Compute b² − 4ac** (the discriminant). Do this as a separate calculation.
4. **Take the square root** of that result.
5. **Plug into the formula:** x = (−b ± √discriminant) / 2a.
6. **Simplify** — split into two answers (+ and −).
7. **Check** at least one answer if time allows.

---

## ✏️ Worked Example

**Solve:** `x² − 5x + 6 = 0`

Step 1: Already standard.
Step 2: a = 1, b = −5, c = 6.
Step 3: Discriminant = b² − 4ac = (−5)² − 4(1)(6) = 25 − 24 = **1**.
Step 4: √1 = 1.
Step 5: x = (−(−5) ± 1) / (2·1) = (5 ± 1) / 2
Step 6: Two answers:
- x = (5 + 1)/2 = 6/2 = **3**
- x = (5 − 1)/2 = 4/2 = **2**

Solutions: **x = 2 or x = 3** ✅

(Quick check: this also factors to `(x−2)(x−3) = 0` → same answers.)

---

## ✏️ Worked Example #2 (uglier)

**Solve:** `2x² + 3x − 1 = 0`

Step 1: Already standard.
Step 2: a = 2, b = 3, c = −1.
Step 3: Discriminant = 3² − 4(2)(−1) = 9 + 8 = **17**.
Step 4: √17 (doesn't simplify — leave it).
Step 5: x = (−3 ± √17) / (2·2) = (−3 ± √17) / 4

Solutions: **x = (−3 + √17)/4 ≈ 0.28** and **x = (−3 − √17)/4 ≈ −1.78**

(This one doesn't factor — the quadratic formula is the only way.)

## ⚠️ Common Traps

1. **Sign errors with b.** The formula starts with **−b**. If b is negative, −b is positive. `b = −5 → −b = +5`. Use parentheses: `−(−5)`.
2. **Order of operations on the discriminant.** Square b BEFORE you do anything else. `b² − 4ac` means `(b·b) − (4·a·c)`.
3. **Forgetting the ±.** Two solutions, always (unless discriminant = 0).
4. **Dividing only PART of the top by 2a.** The whole numerator gets divided. Use a horizontal line.
5. **Trying to do it all in one step.** Break it into 6 numbered steps. Don't be a hero.

1. `x² + 7x + 12 = 0`
2. `x² − 4x + 3 = 0`
3. `2x² + 5x − 3 = 0`
4. `x² + 2x − 8 = 0`
5. `x² + 3x + 1 = 0` (gets a √5 answer — leave in exact form)

## ✅ Answers

1. a=1, b=7, c=12. Disc = 49 − 48 = 1. x = (−7 ± 1)/2 → **x = −3 or x = −4**
2. a=1, b=−4, c=3. Disc = 16 − 12 = 4. x = (4 ± 2)/2 → **x = 3 or x = 1**
3. a=2, b=5, c=−3. Disc = 25 + 24 = 49. x = (−5 ± 7)/4 → **x = ½ or x = −3**
4. a=1, b=2, c=−8. Disc = 4 + 32 = 36. x = (−2 ± 6)/2 → **x = 2 or x = −4**
5. a=1, b=3, c=1. Disc = 9 − 4 = 5. x = (−3 ± √5)/2 → **x = (−3 + √5)/2 or x = (−3 − √5)/2**

(If you got 3+ right → ✓ done. **Problem 5 is the test type — answer stays in exact form.**)

## 🎓 The Discriminant Trick

The discriminant (b² − 4ac) tells you a lot **before** you finish:

| Discriminant value | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Positive** (like 25, 9, 17) | **2 real solutions** |
| **Zero** | **1 real solution** (it's a perfect square, the parabola just touches x-axis) |
| **Negative** | **No real solutions** (in Algebra 1; in higher math, complex solutions) |

If the test asks "how many solutions does this have?" — just compute the discriminant. Don't solve.

## 👨 Nate's Tutor Primer

**This is the safety net** for every solving problem. If she knows nothing else about solving, she can still get any quadratic right with the formula.

When co-working:
- **Memorize the formula cold.** Drill, drill, drill. Write it as the first line of every quadratic-formula problem. Then plug in.
- **The discriminant deserves its own line.** Don't try to compute it inside the formula — write `D = b² − 4ac = ___` separately. Reduces errors.
- **Use parens around negative values.** `b = −5` → write `(−5)²`, not `−5²`. The latter is ambiguous and gets miscomputed.
- **Practice sign substitution.** Show her `x² − 4x + 3 = 0` and ask "what are a, b, c?" Answer: a = 1, b = **−4**, c = 3. The sign goes with the number.

**"New math" note:** Same formula, same name. The "**song version**" is universal across teachers. Khan Academy has a video where Sal sings it — show her.

**Big strategy lesson** (this is the unifying insight of the unit):
- **Factoring** is fastest when factors are obvious.
- **Square roots** is fastest when it's `(stuff)² = number`.
- **Completing the square** is rarely the fastest, but it shows up on tests as a required method.
- **Quadratic formula** ALWAYS works. **When in doubt, use the formula.**

For Esme's test: if she's blanking, the formula is the safety net. **Memorize it like a phone number.**
