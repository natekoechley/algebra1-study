## 🟢 The Big Idea (ELI5)

A **polynomial** is just a bunch of x-terms added together, like `3x² + 5x − 7`.
- Adding two polynomials = **combine the matching pieces.** x²'s with x²'s. x's with x's. Numbers with numbers.
- Subtracting = same thing, but **flip every sign** in the second polynomial first.

That's literally it. The only "trick" is that minus signs love to mess you up — that's the whole skill.

---

## 📖 Vocab You Need

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Term** | A single piece, like `3x²` or `−7` |
| **Coefficient** | The number in front of the variable. In `5x`, the coefficient is **5** |
| **Like terms** | Terms with the **exact same variable AND exponent**. `3x²` and `−5x²` are like terms. `3x²` and `5x` are NOT. |
| **Constant** | A plain number with no variable, like `−7` |
| **Degree** | The biggest exponent in the polynomial |
| **Standard form** | Terms written from biggest exponent to smallest |

---

## 🧾 The Recipe

### Adding: `(polynomial 1) + (polynomial 2)`
1. **Drop the parentheses.** (The + doesn't change any signs.)
2. **Find like terms.** Underline x²'s, circle x's, box the constants.
3. **Add the coefficients of each group.**
4. **Write the answer in standard form** (biggest exponent first).

### Subtracting: `(polynomial 1) − (polynomial 2)`
1. **Flip every sign** in the second polynomial. (+ becomes −, − becomes +.)
2. **Drop the parentheses.**
3. **Find like terms** and **add coefficients.**
4. **Write in standard form.**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic — "FLIP First"

For subtraction: **F**lip the signs, **L**ose the parens, **I**dentify like terms, **P**lus 'em up.

Or even shorter: **"Subtraction = Flip then Add."**

---

## ✏️ Worked Example

**Add:** `(3x² + 5x − 2) + (x² − 4x + 7)`

Step 1: Drop parens → `3x² + 5x − 2 + x² − 4x + 7`
Step 2: Group like terms → `(3x² + x²) + (5x − 4x) + (−2 + 7)`
Step 3: Combine → `4x² + 1x + 5`
Step 4: Tidy → **`4x² + x + 5`** ✅

---

**Subtract:** `(3x² + 5x − 2) − (x² − 4x + 7)`

Step 1: **Flip every sign in the second polynomial** → `−x² + 4x − 7`
Step 2: Now it's an addition → `3x² + 5x − 2 + (−x² + 4x − 7)`
Step 3: Group like terms → `(3x² − x²) + (5x + 4x) + (−2 − 7)`
Step 4: Combine → **`2x² + 9x − 9`** ✅

---

## ⚠️ Common Traps

1. **Forgetting to flip ALL the signs when subtracting** — including the constant! `−(x² − 4x + 7)` becomes `−x² + 4x − 7`, NOT `−x² − 4x + 7`.
2. **Combining un-like terms.** `3x² + 5x` does **not** equal `8x²` or `8x`. They don't combine.
3. **Losing a negative sign in transcription** (the dyscalculia gremlin). Write big, write neat, double-check.
4. **Skipping standard form.** Always write biggest exponent first. Looks pro, easier to grade.

## 👨 Nate's Tutor Primer

**The point of this topic** isn't the math (it's trivial) — it's **building sign-discipline.** Every later topic depends on Esme being calm with negatives.

When co-working:
- Watch for any moment she skips writing the "flip the signs" step. Make her write it.
- Don't let her do it in her head. **Always rewrite the subtracted polynomial with flipped signs as a new line.** This single habit prevents 80% of test errors.
- If she keeps wanting to combine `3x²` with `5x`, ask: "Are the exponents the same?" Don't explain — just ask. Let her notice.

**"New math" note:** Modern textbooks teach this with a vertical alignment style sometimes — stack the polynomials and add columns like elementary-school arithmetic. If she's already using that, **let her.** Don't switch her to your horizontal method.
