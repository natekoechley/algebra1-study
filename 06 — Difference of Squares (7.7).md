## 🟢 The Big Idea (ELI5)

A **difference of squares** is when you see **something² minus something²** — like `x² − 9` or `4x² − 25`.

It always factors into the same pattern:
**`a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b)`**

For `x² − 9`: a = x, b = 3 → `(x + 3)(x − 3)`. Done.

That's literally the whole topic. **Spot it. Apply the pattern. Move on.** It's the easiest factoring trick in the box.

---

## 📖 Vocab You Need

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Perfect square** | A number/term that's something squared. 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100… or x², 4x², 9x² |
| **Difference** | Subtraction (minus) |
| **Sum of squares** | `a² + b²` — **DOES NOT FACTOR** in Algebra 1 (you'll see this in higher math) |

### Perfect Squares You Should Know Cold
| 1² = 1 | 2² = 4 | 3² = 9 | 4² = 16 |
| 5² = 25 | 6² = 36 | 7² = 49 | 8² = 64 |
| 9² = 81 | 10² = 100 | 11² = 121 | 12² = 144 |

## 🧾 The Recipe

For `a² − b²`:

1. **Check GCF first.** (Always.)
2. **Is it a difference of squares?** Check:
   - Two terms?
   - Both perfect squares?
   - **Separated by a minus sign?**
3. **Identify a and b.** (a = square root of first term. b = square root of second term.)
4. **Write the pattern:** `(a + b)(a − b)`
5. **Check by FOIL.**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic — "Spot, Square Root, Split"

1. **S**pot the pattern (perfect² − perfect²)
2. **S**quare **r**oot each piece
3. **S**plit into (sum)(difference)

Or: **"Diff of Squares = Plus times Minus."**

---

## ✏️ Worked Example

**Factor:** `25x² − 49`

Step 1: GCF? No.
Step 2: Difference of squares? Yes — `25x²` is `(5x)²`, `49` is `7²`, minus between them.
Step 3: a = 5x, b = 7.
Step 4: `(5x + 7)(5x − 7)` ✅
Step 5: FOIL: `25x² − 35x + 35x − 49 = 25x² − 49` ✓ (middle terms cancel)

---

## ⚠️ Common Traps

1. **`x² + 9` is NOT a difference of squares.** That's a **sum** of squares — doesn't factor in Algebra 1.
2. **Forgetting GCF.** `8x² − 32` looks like a difference but **isn't of perfect squares** until you factor: `8(x² − 4) = 8(x + 2)(x − 2)`.
3. **The middle term in the FOIL check should cancel.** If it doesn't, you didn't apply diff-of-squares correctly.
4. **Confusing `(a − b)²` with `a² − b²`.** They look similar but are very different. `(a − b)²` = `a² − 2ab + b²` (a *perfect square trinomial*). `a² − b²` = `(a+b)(a−b)`.

## 🎯 Practice Problems (do all 5)

1. `x² − 16`
2. `x² − 100`
3. `9x² − 25`
4. `4x² − 81`
5. `2x² − 50` (hint: GCF first!)

---

## ✅ Answers

1. `(x + 4)(x − 4)`
2. `(x + 10)(x − 10)`
3. `(3x + 5)(3x − 5)`
4. `(2x + 9)(2x − 9)`
5. `2(x + 5)(x − 5)` — pull out 2 first → `2(x² − 25)` → then diff of squares

(If you got 4 or 5 right → ✓ done. This topic should feel **fast** once you spot it.)

## 👨 Nate's Tutor Primer

**This one is mostly about *recognition*.** Once Esme sees the pattern, the work is easy. Spend tutoring time on **spotting**, not on the mechanics.

When co-working:
- **Flash drill:** Show her 5 random binomials. Have her say "Yes diff of squares" or "No." Examples:
  - `x² − 25` ✓
  - `x² + 25` ✗ (plus)
  - `x² − 7` ✗ (7 not a perfect square)
  - `4x² − 81` ✓
  - `(x − 3)²` ✗ (that's a perfect square trinomial, not a diff)
- **Drill the perfect squares table.** Knowing 1²–12² instantly speeds her up massively.
- **Push GCF first habit** — problem #5 is the classic test trap.

**"New math" note:** Identical to how you learned it. The phrase "difference of squares" is universal. Just make sure she also knows the term **"sum of squares does not factor"** — modern tests sometimes include a trick question with `x² + 16` expecting kids to wrongly factor it.

**Pro move:** This is a great topic for confidence-building. Schedule it after a hard topic — she'll get a quick win.
