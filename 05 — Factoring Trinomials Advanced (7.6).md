---

## 🟢 The Big Idea (ELI5)

Same as Topic 4, but harder because the front number isn't 1 anymore.

For `2x² + 7x + 3`, you can't just guess two numbers — the **2** in front breaks the easy pattern. You need a method.

**The trick: the "AC Method."** Multiply a · c, then play the same "two numbers" game with **ac** instead of just c. Then split and group.

---

## 📖 Vocab You Need

| Word | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **AC method** | Multiply a · c, then find two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b |
| **Splitting the middle term** | Rewriting bx as two terms using your found numbers |
| **Factor by grouping** | Group pairs, factor each pair, then factor again |

---

## 🧾 The Recipe — AC Method (the reliable one)

For `ax² + bx + c`:

1. **Check GCF first.** (Always.)
2. **Compute a · c.**
3. **Find two numbers that multiply to (a·c) AND add to b.** (Same game as Topic 4.)
4. **Split the middle term** using those two numbers.
5. **Factor by grouping:** group the first two terms and the last two terms. Factor GCF from each.
6. **If both groups now share the same parens**, factor it out.
7. **Check by FOIL.**

---

## ✏️ Worked Example — AC Method

**Factor:** `2x² + 7x + 3`

Step 1: GCF? No.
Step 2: a · c = 2 · 3 = **6**
Step 3: Two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 7 → **1 and 6**.
Step 4: Split middle term: `2x² + 1x + 6x + 3`
Step 5: Group and factor each pair:
- `(2x² + 1x)` → `x(2x + 1)`
- `(6x + 3)` → `3(2x + 1)`

Step 6: Both groups have `(2x + 1)` → factor it out: **`(2x + 1)(x + 3)`** ✅
Step 7: FOIL check: `2x² + 6x + x + 3 = 2x² + 7x + 3` ✓

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic — "ACE" (A·C, Equal pairs)

**A · C** first. Then play the multiply/add game. Then **E**qual parens come out.

Or: **"a·c, find, split, group, pull."** Say it before every problem.

---

## ⚠️ Common Traps

1. **Forgetting to multiply a·c.** Using just c sends you to wrong numbers.
2. **Splitting the middle term in the wrong order** — actually, **either order works**! Both `1x + 6x` and `6x + 1x` factor correctly. But you must commit and follow through.
3. **Skipping GCF.** `4x² + 14x + 6` → factor out 2 first: `2(2x² + 7x + 3)` = `2(2x + 1)(x + 3)`.
4. **The two groups don't share parens** — if so, you split wrong. Re-check the two numbers.

## 🎯 Practice Problems (do all 5)

1. `2x² + 5x + 2`
2. `3x² + 7x + 2`
3. `2x² − 7x + 3`
4. `5x² + 11x + 2`
5. `2x² + x − 6`

---

## ✅ Answers

1. `(2x + 1)(x + 2)` — ac = 4, need numbers ×4 +5 → 1, 4. Split: `2x² + 1x + 4x + 2 = x(2x+1) + 2(2x+1) = (2x+1)(x+2)`
2. `(3x + 1)(x + 2)` — ac = 6, ×6 +7 → 1, 6. Split: `3x² + 1x + 6x + 2 = x(3x+1) + 2(3x+1) = (3x+1)(x+2)`
3. `(2x − 1)(x − 3)` — ac = 6, ×6 −7 → −1, −6. Split: `2x² − 1x − 6x + 3 = x(2x−1) − 3(2x−1) = (2x−1)(x−3)`
4. `(5x + 1)(x + 2)` — ac = 10, ×10 +11 → 1, 10. Split: `5x² + 1x + 10x + 2 = x(5x+1) + 2(5x+1) = (5x+1)(x+2)`
5. `(2x − 3)(x + 2)` — ac = −12, ×−12 +1 → +4, −3. Split: `2x² + 4x − 3x − 6 = 2x(x+2) − 3(x+2) = (x+2)(2x−3)`

(If you got 3+ right → ✓ done. **If you got 0–2, this needs a Nate tutor session.**)

## 👨 Nate's Tutor Primer

**This is the hardest topic in the factoring section.** Esme almost certainly has this in her test pool because Mr. Mistry called it out (Section 7.6).

When co-working:
- **The AC method is the only reliable approach.** Trial and error works for easy ones but blows up on harder problems. Push the AC method always.
- **Write every step on a new line.** No mental math. Dyscalculia is unkind here.
- **The "split → group → factor pair → match parens" sequence has 4 steps.** Number them on the paper.
- If she's stuck at Step 6 (the parens don't match), the issue is always at Step 3 (wrong two numbers). Go back, try the other sign combo or check your ac calculation.

**"New math" note:** There's another method called "slide and divide" (also "the British method") that some teachers prefer. Mr. Mistry uses AC method (the standard) — stick with that. Don't introduce alternative methods now. **The brain wants one reliable recipe, not three competing ones.**
